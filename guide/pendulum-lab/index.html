Pendulum Lab

A clear route into oscillation, from a simple pendulum to a grandfather clock.

Mixed reality · class board
Open Lesson

Learning Aims

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

  • Measure the period of a pendulum accurately
  • Investigate how length affects the period
  • Describe how energy is transferred and lost as a pendulum swings
  • Apply the ideas to clocks, swings and other planets

Teaching Tips

  • Timing ten swings and dividing by ten is a useful lesson in reducing uncertainty.
  • The giant pendulum hangs behind the bench: encourage students to turn around.
  • The clock challenge works well as a closing task for pairs.

How the Lesson Runs

  1. PredictStudents predict how length and mass affect the swing.
  2. MeasureTen swings are timed by hand and with a light gate, and the length is changed between runs.
  3. EnergyStudents observe how quickly the swing dies away with different bobs and air resistance.
  4. Real WorldA clock is regulated, a swing is pushed and pendulums are tried on other planets.

In a class session, the teacher chooses the activity and level for the whole class from the Teacher Menu.

Running It With a Class

  1. Start the Class SessionOpen the lesson and choose Start a Class Session. The lesson shows a four-character class code and who has joined.
  2. Students Join With the CodeStudents type the code and their name into Joining a Class? at the top of the lessons page. They are taken straight into this lesson and press Enter. When you choose My Bench, each student sees your bench, live, behind their own.
  3. Lead From the Teacher MenuThe Lesson tab sets the activity for everyone, and the Board tab chooses what stands behind every bench: nothing at the start, the class board or your own bench, live. The Students tab shows progress and can show one student’s work to everyone; the Class tab holds the class tools.

Class Tools

  • Pause the Class dims every student’s view and pauses their controls while you explain something. Resume the Class brings everything back.
  • Look Here points every student towards you for eight seconds, with an arrow and a ring above your head.
  • Timer counts down one, two, five or ten minutes near the top of everyone’s view, with a chime at the end.
  • A headset that sleeps or loses the Wi-Fi rejoins by itself and keeps its work. Students who join late receive the current settings, pause and timer at once.
  • Students can use their hands instead of controllers: pinch or close a hand to pick things up and touch a button to press it. Turning a palm towards the face shows two buttons for hiding the menus and moving the lab.
