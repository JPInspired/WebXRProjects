Heart Lab

A beating human heart on every desk, with a larger one for the whole class that the teacher controls.

30 minAges 11–16 · KS3–KS4Mixed reality · class heart
Open Lesson Test It on a Headset

Learning Aims

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

  • Name the chambers, valves and blood vessels of the heart
  • Describe the path of blood through the heart
  • Relate the cardiac cycle to an ECG
  • Explain how exercise and disease affect the heart

Teaching Tips

  • Use the class heart to demonstrate before students explore their own.
  • Freeze the cardiac cycle at each stage and ask which valves are open.
  • In a same-room session, place the class heart in the middle of the room.

How the Lesson Runs

  1. ExploreStudents turn and open their own heart and read about each part.
  2. Blood FlowBlood is followed through the chambers and valves.
  3. Cardiac CycleEach stage of a heartbeat is shown alongside a live ECG.
  4. Exercise and ConditionsStudents see how exercise and heart disease change the heart’s work.
  5. Label ItEvery part is labelled as a closing check.

In a shared session, the teacher chooses the activity and level for the whole class from the Teacher Menu.

Running It With a Class

  1. Start the SessionOpen the lesson, choose Start a Shared Session and tick We are in the same room. The lesson shows a four-character code.
  2. Students Join With the CodeStudents type the code and their name into Joining a Class? at the top of the lessons page. They are taken straight into this lesson, lined up with the room.
  3. Lead From the Teacher MenuThe Lesson tab sets the activity for everyone, the Students tab shows progress and the Class tab holds the class tools.

Class Tools

  • Pause the Class dims every student’s view and pauses their controls while you explain something. Resume the Class brings everything back.
  • Look Here points every student towards you for eight seconds, with an arrow and a ring above your head.
  • Timer counts down one, two, five or ten minutes near the top of everyone’s view, with a chime at the end.
  • A headset that sleeps or loses the Wi-Fi rejoins by itself and keeps its work. Students who join late receive the current settings, pause and timer at once.
  • Students can use their hands instead of controllers: pinch or close a hand to pick things up and touch a button to press it. Turning a palm towards the face shows two buttons for hiding the menus and moving the lab.
  • Before the first shared lesson in a new room, run the Network Check on the school Wi-Fi.
